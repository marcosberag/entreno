Training ·
Plan rules & the reasoning behind them
  • Leave 1-2 reps in reserve on every working set. Stopping 1-2 reps short of failure drives essentially the same hypertrophy as going to failure, with far less fatigue — which matters when you train 5 days a week.
  • Hit the top of the rep range on every set → make the exercise harder, don't add reps. Each card tells you how. With 10 kg dumbbells you will run out of load long before you run out of stimulus, so progression here is mechanical: leverage, tempo, range and pauses.
  • Rest longer than feels necessary. 2-3 min on legs, pull-ups and presses; 60-90 s on isolation work. Short rests cost you reps, and lost reps cost you growth — longer rests produce more hypertrophy and strength, not less.
  • Light loads still build muscle — as long as the set ends close to failure. That is the whole basis of this program: your 10 kg dumbbells are plenty, provided you take the hard sets seriously.
  • Two sessions per muscle group per week. Training a muscle twice weekly beats once weekly when total volume is equal, which is why legs land on Monday and Friday rather than in one long session.
  • First two weeks: do 3 sets where it says 4. Start below your capacity so you have somewhere to go.
  • Every 6-8 weeks, take a lighter week: half the sets, same effort per set. Fatigue masks fitness; you often look stronger the week after backing off.
  • Thursday and Sunday are training too. Growth happens between sessions, not during them. Walk, sleep, eat. If you feel wrecked on Saturday, that session is the one to cut.
Evidence base: Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger, Sports Med 2016 (frequency) and J Sports Sci 2017 (volume dose-response); Schoenfeld et al., J Strength Cond Res 2017 (low vs high load) and 2016 (rest intervals); Morton et al., Br J Sports Med 2018 (protein); Helms et al., IJSNEM 2014 (protein in a deficit).
